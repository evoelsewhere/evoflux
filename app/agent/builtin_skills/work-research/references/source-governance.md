# Source governance

Use this reference for consequential, contested, estimated, or current claims.

## Source preference

1. Governing text, official standard, regulator, court, or first-party current
   documentation for what a rule or product says.
2. Original dataset, filing, research paper, or methodology for measured
   claims.
3. Independent expert analysis for interpretation and limitations.
4. Reputable reporting for events and discovery.
5. Aggregators, snippets, forums, and social posts only as leads unless the
   post itself is the subject.

Authority is claim-specific. A vendor is authoritative about its documented
price, not about being the best product. A survey is authoritative about its
sample, not automatically the whole market.

## Evidence ledger

| Claim | Source | Date/version | Scope/method | Independence | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exact statement | Stable link or workspace record | Relevant date | Population and definition | Original / derived / repeated | high / medium / low |

## Contradictions

Compare definitions, measurement windows, population, geography, version, and
publication incentives. Prefer the source whose method matches the question.
If the conflict remains material, present both and state what evidence would
resolve it.

## Citation audit

- Open the final source, not only a search result or quoted secondary source.
- Attach citations immediately after the supported claim.
- Do not cite a source for a stronger claim than it makes.
- Label paywalled, archived, translated, or inaccessible evidence.
- Preserve exact dates for changing facts and distinguish publication date
  from the period measured.
